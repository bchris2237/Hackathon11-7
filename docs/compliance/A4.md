## Design explanation

### New module: `optimizer/metrics.py`

A4 adds explicit `compute_metrics` and `rank_allocation` functions with clean separation of concerns:

**`compute_metrics(scenario, assignment_map) → (list[BusinessMetrics], Totals)`**

Applies the frozen contract formulas to one specific assignment map and returns structured output. Deliberately does NOT call the solver or reuse engine internals — it is an independent implementation of the same accounting rules:

- `baseline_i` = sum of owner's estimates for own deliverables
- `production_i` = sum of provider's estimate for each deliverable assigned to i
- `involved` = business participates in any cross-business assignment (as provider or as owner whose work was outsourced)
- `coordinationCharge_i` = `coordinationMinutes` once if involved, else 0
- `effort_i` = production + coordination
- `netSavings_i` = baseline - effort

**Important model limitation stated explicitly:** Savings are minutes of effort, not monetary utility. Two businesses saving equal minutes may have very different cash implications. This is documented in the module docstring and must be communicated to judges.

**`rank_allocation(candidates) → list[ScoredCandidate]`**

Takes a list of `(assignment_map, metrics, totals)` triples and:
1. **Filters** any candidate where `any(m.netSavingsMinutes < 0)` — enforces no-worse-off at the scoring stage, independent of whether total savings are positive.
2. **Sorts** survivors best-first by the three-level tiebreak key.

The no-worse-off filter at this stage is what prevents a globally attractive allocation from silently harming a participant. Total net savings being positive is necessary but not sufficient.

### Tiebreak key (`_score_key`)

All integer arithmetic, no division:

1. `totals.effortMinutes` (ascending)
2. `max(effort_i × total_baseline)` as proxy for `max(effort_i / baseline_i)` (ascending). Zero baseline → `10^15` sentinel.
3. `sorted(assignment_map.items())` lexicographically (ascending).

### ScoredCandidate

A `NamedTuple` with `assignment_map`, `metrics`, `totals`, `score_key`. Returned from `rank_allocation` in best-first order.

## Judge questions

**Q1: Why filter out harmed businesses in `rank_allocation` rather than in the solver?**  
A: Defense in depth. The solver already filters infeasible candidates, but `rank_allocation` is the public scoring API — any caller (backend simulate handler, future extensions) that constructs candidates independently must get the same no-worse-off guarantee without knowing the solver's internals. The filter at the scoring stage makes the constraint explicit and independently testable.

**Q2: How does the model account for businesses with very different hourly rates?**  
A: It doesn't. The model measures labor burden in minutes, not monetary value. Two businesses saving 300 minutes may have completely different cash implications depending on wage rates, opportunity costs and market conditions — none of which are captured. The certificate states this explicitly in its assumptions. Judges should be told the model proves effort arithmetic under supplied estimates, not commercial profit.

**Q3: Why is coordination charged once per business, not once per assignment edge?**  
A: The contract spec states "charge its coordinationMinutes once if i provides to or receives from a different business." This models the real overhead of setting up cross-business communication (contracts, onboarding, meetings) — which is a fixed cost incurred by joining an exchange, not proportional to how many tasks are exchanged. The implementation checks `involved` (boolean) per business, not counts of cross-edges.
