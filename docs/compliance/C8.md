## Findings that were real, and what was done

Found by axe (before any change): the graph was `role="img"` with focusable descendants (nested-interactive, serious); the two certificate sections shared one landmark name once a what-if was on screen (landmark-unique). Both fixed: the graph is now a `role="group"` whose nodes and edges are not focusable, and the certificate and explanation boxes are `role="group"`, not landmarks.

Found by my own browser checks (axe did not report them):
- At 360 px the page scrolled sideways by 153 px once results were shown. Cause: the grid columns and fieldsets grew to the table's minimum width. Fixed with `min-w-0` on both panels and every fieldset.
- At 320 px long field paths in the extraction preview forced 9 px of overflow. Fixed by letting `code` wrap.
- Tables that still overflow at 360 px were mouse-only scroll areas. New `ScrollRegion`: when content really overflows it becomes a labelled, focusable group (arrow keys scroll) with a visible "Scroll sideways" hint. No tab stop and no hint when everything fits.
- The banner's mode button was under 24 px tall. Now at least 32 px.
- Two graphs on screen produced duplicate DOM ids (React Flow derives ids from an instance id). Each graph now gets a unique `id`.
- Pressing Optimize, Confirm, Extract, Propose, Apply or Explain disabled the button while the request ran, which makes the browser drop keyboard focus to the page body. These now use `aria-disabled` (still focusable, clicks ignored by `useLatestRequest`), and the Simulation fieldsets no longer disable while busy. Real `disabled` is kept only when an action is genuinely unavailable (nothing confirmed yet). The retry button during a Retry-After wait is `aria-disabled` too, so it can be found and its countdown read.
- Focus was lost when the pressed element disappeared. Now: a model proposal moves focus to its review heading; Apply moves focus to the what-if result heading; Cancel and Discard move it to the "What-if simulation" heading; removing a manual change moves it to "Add change"; a failed action moves focus to the error box (except the automatic health check on load, which must not steal focus); a bad manual value focuses the field with the problem and links the error with `aria-describedby`.
- The optimization result had no spoken announcement when focus stayed on the button. Added a visually hidden polite live region ("Optimization finished. Beneficial exchange found.").
- The skip link now lands focus in `<main>` (`tabIndex=-1`).
- A typo, "verification..", in the fixture certificate text.

Seen by looking at the screenshots and fixed: the exchange graph drew curves looping around the boxes, arrowheads were barely visible, and two edge labels sat under nodes. The graph now has a custom edge: straight arrows between box borders, a clear arrowhead, a sideways bow only when two arrows share a pair, and labels placed just outside the triangle. The business box ids and `edge-<deliverableId>` ids are unchanged.

Checked and dismissed: my first contrast check read Tailwind v4 `oklch()` colours as text and reported 2.71:1 for the focus ring on the amber banner. I added a CSS override for it, then replaced the check with a canvas-based sRGB conversion, found the real ratio is about 4.6:1, and removed the override. The ring is still white on the green banner, where the blue ring would be about 1.2:1.

## What was actually checked

States: blank form, sample loaded, extraction review, optimized (graph, table, certificate), explanation shown, text proposal under review, applied what-if, optimize error, health error, live-mode banner. Each at 1280 x 900 and 360 x 740 for axe, and at 360 and 320 for layout. A 640 px window stands in for 200 % zoom.

Layout rule: no page overflow, every button, field, select, textarea, link and focusable group within the viewport, and at least 24 x 24 px (the skip link and the graph library's credit link are excluded).

Keyboard walkthrough: Tab to skip link and activate it; Load sample (Enter); Confirm (Space); Optimize (Enter); pick a business by typing; enter 10; Add change; Apply; Discard. At every Tab stop the test asserts forward document order and a drawn focus ring of at least 2 px. A separate test Tabs through the page with a what-if open and shows focus leaves the page (no trap).

## Assumptions and limits

- axe cannot find every WCAG problem; it cannot judge reading order, the meaning of labels, or screen-reader behaviour. The text of hints and headlines was not user-tested.
- Everything ran in Chromium with the API stubbed through `page.route` (hand-written responses, see `support.ts`). None of it touches a real backend, Bedrock or AWS.
- The graph is small at 360 px (about 8 to 9 px text after fitting). The list below it carries the same information at normal size and is the accessible alternative.
- The Confirm and Optimize buttons are truly `disabled` while their prerequisites are missing, so a keyboard user cannot focus them; the reason is stated in text next to them. This was left as is.
- The mode switch changes what the app accepts; it does not change the backend (see C7).
- Complexity: the checks are constant-time per screen; `ScrollRegion` measures on resize, deferred one frame.

## AWS and cross-owner requests

No AWS use in the browser. For Developer 2 (not edited by me): serve the SPA so that any path returns `index.html` with status 200. In Amplify Hosting this is a rewrite rule, whose current form is in the official page [Redirects and rewrites example reference](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/amplify/latest/userguide/redirect-rewrite-examples.html). This app has no client-side routes yet, so only `/` is used today; the rule matters if a link or refresh hits another path. Also set `VITE_API_BASE_URL` and `VITE_DATA_MODE` for the Amplify build, add the Amplify origin to the API's allowed origin, and (from C7) expose `Retry-After` through CORS.

## Judge questions

1. How do you know it is accessible? Automated axe scans in 20 states, a keyboard-only run through the full flow, layout checks at 360 and 320 px, and focus rules, all as repeatable tests; plus a list of what we did not do (no screen-reader or device testing).
2. What bug mattered most? Pressing a button disabled it, and the browser then dropped keyboard focus to the page. It affected every action. Buttons now stay focusable with `aria-disabled`, and focus moves deliberately when an element disappears.
3. Is the graph the only way to see the result? No. Every arrow is also a sentence in the list below ("DesignCo does d-design (design, 180 min (3 h)) for DevCo."), the graph has no tab stops, and the results table and certificate carry the numbers.
4. Does it work on a phone? At 360 and 320 px nothing overflows or gets clipped and targets are at least 24 px; wide tables scroll sideways with a hint and keyboard access. We have not tried a real device.
